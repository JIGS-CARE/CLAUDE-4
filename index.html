    JIGS Quality: the standard every family can check

    Every home on JIGS CARE is licensed by the state. JIGS Quality homes go further: they meet ten published standards for safety, honesty and person-centered care, and we recheck them every 90 days. No home can buy the badge. It's free to earn on any plan.

    The ten standards

      How the badge works

      Rechecked every 90 days

      Standards are reviewed every quarter. If a home falls short, it gets 30 days to fix it before the badge comes off.

      Suspended right away

      If DSHS takes enforcement action, such as a stop-placement order or a license condition, the badge is suspended the same day until it's resolved.

      Used honestly

      Homes may say they "meet the JIGS Quality standards" and must link the badge to this page. They may not call themselves "the best" or imply the state endorses the badge.

      Where homes stand today

      JIGS Quality opens with the first claimed homes. Until a home applies and passes, its listing says "Not yet assessed". That's not a mark against it, just an honest status.

      How to choose an adult family home

      An adult family home is a licensed home in a regular neighborhood where a small number of adults, six or fewer in most homes and up to eight in some, live and get daily care. Here's a simple way to choose well.

      1. Know the care you need

        Write down help needed with bathing, dressing, medicines, memory or behavior, and mobility. If dementia, mental health or a developmental disability is part of it, use those filters: they show homes with that state specialty.

        Paying with Medicaid? Filter for Medicaid contract, and apply for state-funded care at your local Home and Community Services office.

      2. Check the license and inspection history

        Every JIGS CARE listing links to the home's record on the Washington DSHS site, including inspection reports. Homes must also keep their inspection reports from the past three years available for you to read, so ask to see them.

      3. Tour two or three homes

        Visit at different times of day if you can. Questions worth asking:

        • Who is awake and on duty at night?
        • What training and credentials do caregivers have (HCA, NAC)?
        • What languages are spoken?
        • How are medicines handled and recorded?
        • What does the monthly cost include, and what costs extra?
        • What happens if care needs increase?
        • How much notice is given before a move-out, and why might that happen?
        • What does a typical day look like: meals, activities, outings?
        • How do you keep families updated?

      4. Get free, local help

        Community Living Connections (your local Area Agency on Aging) offers free advice on care options and benefits: waclc.org/connect.

        Concerns about care in a home? The Washington Long-Term Care Ombudsman helps residents and families for free: waombudsman.org.

        The state's own booklet, Choosing Care in an Adult Family Home or Assisted Living Facility, goes deeper.

      Never miss a background check deadline

      Every caregiver needs a DSHS fingerprint check within 120 days of starting care, and a Washington name and date of birth check renewed every two years. JIGS CARE tracks both for every caregiver, free, and warns you before an inspector finds a gap.
